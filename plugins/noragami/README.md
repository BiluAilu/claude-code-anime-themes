# Noragami

An anime-RPG look for the Claude desktop app, built from original silhouette art inspired by Noragami: three different worlds and four main characters as playable companions (Yato, Hiyori, Yukine and Bishamon), each with four traced poses and their own signature effects. The art was generated by the author and traced to compact vector paths with `tools/trace.mjs` (`hooks/art.ts` and `hooks/chars.ts` hold the paths; the source images are not shipped). No screenshots or promotional art from the series are used.

## The three worlds
- **Shrine Night** (default): a big torii under a crescent moon, a small shrine, stone lanterns, rooftops and drifting ghost fire in midnight blue. Offerings (coins) pile up in front of the shrine, one per real tool call (up to 14). Phantoms creep in when tools fail.
- **Far Shore**: the underworld river in murky teal and lantern red. A half-sunk torii with its reflection, glowing lanterns, tall phantoms at the edges and small phantoms drifting with glowing eyes.
- **Takamagahara**: a golden sun inside a ring of clouds behind a heavenly gate, on a floor of drifting clouds, in indigo and gold.

Each world has its own palette for the whole interface (surfaces, text, accent, git colors, code highlighting) and its own message box shape.

## The character sets the look
Choosing a character re-themes the whole app, not only the companion. The world is the scenery and base palette; the character supplies:
- the interface accent and a secondary color (buttons, links, code keywords, focus),
- a slight tint on every surface and on the frosted glass,
- the color of message box edges and headings,
- a shift of the backdrop's lights toward their color, plus their aura and effects.

So Gon in Godspeed turns the interface green while the world is still Killua's cyan-lit rooftop, and switching the world keeps the character's colors. Every world and character combination is checked to be a theme the desktop will paint.

## The cast and real activity
You choose who you play as; the others join you when subagents start. What the companion does follows real events only:
| Session event | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Idle | The character stands, breathing, glowing in their own color |
| Thinking | A rune ring spins around their head |
| Text streaming | Their action pose (weapon, fist or claws raised) |
| Tool call | A dash with afterimage and their signature effect; the kind of tool changes the effect |
| Turn completes | They sit or kneel, one flash and an expanding ring, then calm after 5 s |
| Tool failure or API error | Red vignette and cracks; phantoms gather in Shrine Night and Far Shore |
| Context 80% or more | Amber vignette; toast at 80% and again at 90% |
| Subagent starts or stops | The next main character in the roster joins you (one silhouette each, in their own color); the toast names them and the band shows `party N` |
| Permission prompt shown | They sit, amber glow and rings, "awaiting your word"; ends when the tool finishes |
| Context compaction | Rings contract inward and the sky darkens; a toast shows the real before and after percentage |
| Tool kind (real tool name) | Edit/Write: the character's signature strike (below). Read/Grep/Glob: sonar rings. WebFetch/WebSearch: light beam. Agent/Task: summon rings. MCP: cyan lightning. Bash and others: dash |

Signature effects for edits and dashes:
- **Yato** (ice blue): two sword arcs for edits, a dash with a slash.
- **Hiyori** (pink): flowing ribbons of light for edits and dashes.
- **Yukine** (silver-blue): one bright blade stroke with sparks for edits, a dash with a slash.
- **Bishamon** (gold): a wide sweeping sword wave for edits, a dash with a slash.

Counts, the context percentage and the turn length shown in toasts come from the engine (`tool.call`, `turn.step`, `turn.complete`, the classic subagent, permission and compaction events, and `$.session.usage()`). The only timer returns the scene to calm 5 s (6.5 s after an error) after a finish. Nothing is simulated.

## Status band
`◆ WORLD ◆ Character · state │ turns N · tools N · fails N · party N │ ▰▰▰▱▱▱▱▱▱▱ 30%`. The context meter turns amber at 80%.

## Commands
- `/noragami` shows the current world and character.
- `/noragami <world>` switches the world (shrine, farshore, heaven); `/noragami <character>` switches who you play as (yato, hiyori, yukine, bishamon). `who` and `world` can be written out: `/noragami who hiyori`. Both are remembered across sessions.
- `/noragami status` prints the real event counts this session received.
- `/noragami off` returns the app's own look; `/noragami on` brings the theme back.

## Install and rollback
Install from a folder with `claude --plugin-dir <this folder>`, or from a marketplace that lists it. Rollback: `/noragami off`, uninstall, or delete the folder; your settings are never changed. A session has one theme at a time, so a theme mod loaded later replaces this look until you run `/noragami on` again.

## What works and what does not
Works (tested): all three worlds on desktop and mobile, every character in every state, the commands, the real-event counters, toasts, the band on terminal and desktop, and text contrast (primary at least 9.2:1; muted about 4.3:1 or better against the brightest backdrop pixels; framed messages 15:1). The largest scene across every world, character, state and tool kind is under 115,000 of the 131,072 characters allowed.

Not supported or not done:
- The terminal and editors draw no theme or backdrop, only the band.
- No sound.
- Scene changes redraw the scene, so the animation restarts on each state change; there is no cross-fade.
- The companion sits in the left margin and is cropped on narrow windows.
- A permission wait ends when the tool finishes, because the engine has no separate event for the moment of approval.
- Party members come from the subagent start and stop events; at most one silhouette per other character is drawn (so three), while the band counts every running subagent. If the engine does not fire those events in some mode, nobody arrives.
- Reduced motion: the app shows the scene at rest.
- Tests exercise the hooks against the documented event shapes; whether your app version draws mod backdrops can only be confirmed by looking.
